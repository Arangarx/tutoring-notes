# Tutor interview guide

## Purpose

These interviews exist to find out what tutors really want and need. They help us make the site the best it can be, and better than other tools. They are not a way to confirm that what we already built is the best. It is fine to learn whether people like what we built. Do not lead them there. The current pilot's history is in `docs/SARAH-CALL-PREP.md`. Use it as background. Do not treat one tutor's answers as the market.

## How to interview

Ask about real past behavior. "Tell me about the last time you..." is the shape. Open questions, not yes or no. Do not name our features or describe our solution before they describe their problem. Ask what they do today, what workarounds they use, what they pay for, and what they gave up on. Follow up with "why" and "what happened next." Let silence sit. Ask for examples. Save any reaction to our product for the end. Ask it neutrally. "What, if anything, would you change?" is the shape. "Do you like this?" is not.

## Leading and neutral

Read this before the topic sections. Do not read it aloud.

Leading: "Would a shared board that records the lesson and writes the parent recap for you solve your follow-up?"

Neutral: "Tell me about the last session you taught. What did you use during it, and what did you do after it ended?"

## How they find and keep students

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): where students actually come from, what makes a family stay, and what makes one leave. We want their current channels and the ones they stopped using.

- Tell me about the last student you started with. How did they find you?
- What do you do, in a normal month, to get the next student?
- Tell me about a student or family who left. What happened before that?
- Which ways of finding students have you stopped using? What made you stop?

Probes: What did that cost you in time or money? What did you try instead? What happened next?

## How a session runs today

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): the real sequence of a session, including in person and online, and where they improvise. We want the tools in their hands, not a wish list.

- Walk me through the last session you taught, from the moment you sat down to the moment you were done.
- What is different when you teach in person, compared with online? Use the last time you did each.
- What broke, stalled, or felt clumsy in that session? What did you do about it?
- Who else is in the room or on the call, and what do they do?

Probes: Why that tool? What happened next? Can you show me, or tell me the exact steps?

## Preparing and following up

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): the work before and after the paid hour, who it is for, and which parts they have tried to shrink.

- Tell me about the last session you prepared for. What did you actually prepare, and how long did it take?
- After that session, what did you do before you were finished with that student for the day?
- What follow-up have you dropped because it took too long? What did you do instead?
- Who asks you for something after the session, and what do they ask for?

Probes: Why that, and not something shorter? What happened when you skipped it?

## Notes and what parents get

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): what a note is for in their real work, who reads it, and what "good enough" looks like. We want examples of notes they send, notes they keep private, and notes they stopped writing.

- Tell me about the last note or recap you wrote. Who was it for, and what did you put in it?
- What do parents actually read or reply to? Use a recent example.
- What are you required to write, by a school, a platform, or a parent, that you would not write for yourself?
- Tell me about a time the notes were wrong, late, or unused. What happened?

Probes: How long did that take? What did you copy from last time? What would you have skipped if nobody was checking?

## Recording and consent

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): whether they record today, what they tell families, what families refuse, and what they do when someone says no. Do not describe our consent switches.

- Tell me about the last time you recorded a session, or decided not to. What did you record, and with what?
- What do you say to a parent or a student before any recording happens?
- Tell me about a time someone did not want to be recorded. What did you do in that session?
- Where do those recordings live now, and who can open them?

Probes: Why did you want the recording? What happened to it afterward? What are you not willing to record?

## Scheduling and calendars

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): how a time gets onto a calendar, what they do when it moves, and which calendar they trust. We want the last reschedule, not a hypothetical calendar product.

- Tell me how the last session got onto your calendar. Who proposed the time?
- Tell me about the last time a session moved or was cancelled. What did you update, and what did you forget?
- What calendar or list do you look at on the day? What else are you copying the time into?
- What scheduling help have you paid for or turned off? Why?

Probes: What went wrong the last time two things overlapped? What do students and parents see?

## Payments and billing

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): who pays, how time is counted, what they round, and what they fight about. We want a recent invoice or platform payout, not a pricing opinion in the abstract.

- Tell me about the last time you were paid for tutoring. Who paid, and how was the amount decided?
- How do you count the time? Use the last session that did not run the full hour.
- What do you report to a school, a platform, or a parent about time and attendance?
- Tell me about a payment or a timesheet that went wrong. What did you have to fix?

Probes: What do you round to? What are you not allowed to bill? What do you still write down by hand?

## Tools they use and why

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): the stack they already pay for and the jobs each tool does. We want what they chose, what was chosen for them, and what they resent.

- What did you open during the last session, besides the video call? Why that, for each one?
- What do you pay for monthly that is mainly for tutoring? What job does each one do?
- What were you told to use, by a school or a platform, that you would not have chosen?
- What have you tried and stopped using in the last year? What was the last straw?

Probes: What does that tool replace? What do you still do outside it?

## What wastes their time

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): repeated work they resent, in their words, from a recent week. We want frequency and who the work is for.

- In the last week, what tutoring work felt like a waste of time? Walk me through one example.
- What do you retype, reformat, or send twice?
- What do you do only because someone else requires the format?
- What problem have you stopped trying to fix?

Probes: How often is that? What did you try? Why did you give up?

## What they would pay for

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): money they already spend, and the job they would pay to make go away. We want a past purchase, not a reaction to a feature list.

- What was the last thing you paid for to make tutoring easier? Why that?
- What do you do yourself that you would pay someone, or a tool, to take off your plate? What is that worth, compared with what you pay now?
- What have you refused to pay for? Why?
- If you had to drop one paid tool this month, which one, and what would you go back to doing by hand?

Probes: Who approves that spending, you or someone else? What did you expect it to do that it did not do?

## Trust and privacy concerns

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): what they are afraid of losing, leaking, or being blamed for. We want a real worry they have acted on.

- What student or family information are you careful about, and where do you refuse to put it?
- Tell me about a time you did not want a recording, a note, or a name stored somewhere. What did you do?
- What would you need to believe before you put a minor's session in a new tool?
- Who do parents ask you about trust, and what do you tell them?

Probes: What went wrong, or nearly went wrong? What did you stop using because of that?

## Switching costs

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): what would make a move painful, including history, parents, calendars, and getting paid. We want the last time they actually switched, or refused to.

- Tell me about the last time you moved tutoring work from one tool to another. What did you have to bring with you?
- What would you lose if you stopped using your main tool this month?
- What have families or schools gotten used to that you would have to replace?
- What almost made you switch, and what stopped you?

Probes: How long did the last move take? What did you leave behind on purpose?

## Our open product questions

Ask these last, after their own account. State the situation in one plain sentence, then ask what they would change. Do not ask them to praise it.

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): their reaction to a few choices we still need real tutors to pressure-test. A "nothing" is an answer. Write it down.

- A session can be ended before the tutor is finished, for example because they left an organization. What should happen to the notes from that session? What, if anything, would you still need to write?
- If you stopped working with a school or a company in the middle of the week, what would you still need access to, and for how long?
- We limit a private tutor to 20 new students and 30 claim emails a day. What, if anything, would you change about a limit like that?
- What, if anything, would you change about a tool that records a session only when the family has agreed, and lets them turn pieces of that off later?

Probes: Tell me about a time that situation happened. What did you do then?
