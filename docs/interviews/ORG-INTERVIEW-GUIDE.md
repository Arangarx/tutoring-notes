# Organization interview guide

## Purpose

These interviews exist to find out what tutoring organizations really want and need. They help us make the site the best it can be, and better than other tools. They are not a way to confirm that what we already built is the best. It is fine to learn whether people like what we built. Do not lead them there. Ask the people who decide and the people who would use it. Those are often not the same person.

## How to interview

Ask about real past behavior. "Tell me about the last time you..." is the shape. Open questions, not yes or no. Do not name our features or describe our solution before they describe their problem. Ask what they do today, what workarounds they use, what they pay for, and what they gave up on. Follow up with "why" and "what happened next." Let silence sit. Ask for examples. Save any reaction to our product for the end. Ask it neutrally. "What, if anything, would you change?" is the shape. "Do you like this?" is not.

## Leading and neutral

Read this before the topic sections. Do not read it aloud.

Leading: "When you remove a tutor mid-session, do you want us to keep their notes as read-only?"

Neutral: "Tell me about the last time a tutor stopped working with you while students were still scheduled. What happened to those sessions and those notes?"

## Who decides and who uses it

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): who can require a tool, who can ignore it, and who feels the daily pain. We want the real chain, including people who are not in the room.

- Who would have to say yes before your tutors used a new tool? Who can say no?
- Who would actually live in it each week? Who would only look at reports?
- Tell me about the last tool you rolled out to tutors. Who pushed it, and who worked around it?
- What decision about tutoring tools is not yours to make?

Probes: What happened when someone refused? Who got blamed?

## How they assign and schedule tutors

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): how a student gets a tutor and a time, including more than one tutor, substitutes, and last-minute changes. We want the last real week, not an org chart.

- Tell me how the last new student got a tutor and a first time on the calendar.
- Tell me about the last time you changed who teaches a student. What did you have to update?
- How often does one student see more than one tutor? How do you keep that straight?
- What scheduling work still happens in email, a spreadsheet, or a group chat?

Probes: What broke the last time two sessions overlapped? What do tutors see, and what do families see?

## Onboarding and removing tutors

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): the real steps to add a tutor and the real steps to take one off, including a tutor who is in the middle of work. Save our removal choices for the last section.

- Walk me through the last tutor you brought on. What did they need before they could see a student?
- Walk me through the last tutor you removed. What did you turn off, and what did you leave in place?
- Tell me about a removal that happened while that tutor still had a session coming, or a session in progress. What did you do that day?
- What access have you regretted leaving on, or regretted cutting off too fast?

Probes: Who is allowed to remove someone? What did families hear, and when?

## What they need to see, and what they must not see

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): the difference between running the schedule and reading a student's private record. We want a time someone saw too much, and a time someone could not see enough.

- On a normal day, what do you need to see about a student in order to do your job?
- What should a scheduler be able to see? What should they not see?
- Tell me about a time someone in your organization saw something they should not have. What was it, and what did you change?
- Tell me about a time you could not get a fact you needed. What did you do instead?

Probes: Does that answer change for an owner, a scheduler, and a tutor? Why?

## Parents and consent

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): who agrees, for a child and for an adult student, and what happens when permission is missing or withdrawn. Do not describe our consent page.

- Tell me about the last family you set up. Who did you contact, the student or a parent, and what did you ask them to agree to?
- What do you do when the student is an adult and agrees for themselves? What is different for a child?
- Tell me about a time consent was late, refused, or taken back. What happened to the sessions?
- What proof do you keep that someone agreed? Where does it live?

Probes: Who is allowed to change that agreement later? What do you tell a tutor who wants to start anyway?

## Reporting

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): the reports they already produce, who demands them, and what they assemble by hand. We want the last real report, not a dashboard wish.

- What did you have to report in the last month, and to whom?
- Walk me through how you built the last one. What did you pull from, and what did you retype?
- What do you report only because a school, a payer, or a contract requires it?
- What do you wish a report showed that you cannot get today? What do you do instead?

Probes: How long did that take? What was wrong with the last version you sent?

## Billing and how time should count

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): what time is billable, including short sessions, no-shows, early ends, and overtime. We want their current rule and a recent dispute. Do not ask them to bless a rule we already picked until the last section.

- How do you decide what to bill for a session? Use the last invoice or timesheet.
- Tell me about a session that did not run the planned length. What got billed, and why?
- What time are you not allowed to bill? Who enforces that?
- Tell me about a billing disagreement. What was the argument, and how did it end?

Probes: What do you round to? What do you still calculate in a spreadsheet?

## When a tutor leaves, or a session ends early

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): what they did the last time work stopped in the middle. Notes, families, pay, and the next tutor. The questions we still owe are in the last section. Do not preview them here.

- Tell me about the last session that ended earlier than planned. What happened to the rest of that hour, the notes, and the pay?
- Tell me about the last tutor who left with students still on the books. What did those students experience the next day?
- What did you expect that tutor to finish after they were no longer teaching? What did they actually finish?
- What fell through the cracks? How did you find out?

Probes: Who told the family? What did the next tutor receive?

## Notes and who owns them

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): who is allowed to write, edit, read, and keep notes, and what happens to notes when a tutor or a family leaves. We want ownership in practice, not a policy sentence they have not used.

- Who is allowed to write or change a session note? Who is only allowed to read it?
- Tell me about the last time you needed a note the tutor had written. Could you open it? Should you have been able to?
- When a tutor leaves, whose notes are those? What have you actually done with them?
- What notes should a parent or a student see? What should stay with the tutor or with you?

Probes: Tell me about a fight over a note. What was the outcome?

## Removed-tutor access

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): what a tutor who has been removed can still open, in their current practice, and what they wish were true. Our current default, read-only rather than a full cutoff, is a question for the last section. Do not describe it here.

- After a tutor has been removed, what can they still see today? What can they no longer see?
- Tell me about a removed tutor who still needed to finish something. What did you let them do?
- Tell me about a time you needed them cut off completely. What was the reason, and what did you do?
- What would have gone wrong if they kept reading old sessions? What would have gone wrong if they could not?

Probes: How long did that access last? Who decided?

## Caps and limits

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): real volume in a day, and where a limit would block honest work or would be a relief. Our numbers are in the last section.

- On your busiest day recently, how many new students, invite emails, and staff invites went out? What was that day?
- What limits do you already set on tutors or on staff? What were they for?
- Tell me about a time someone sent too many emails, or added people too fast. What happened?
- What limit would have stopped real work you needed to do?

Probes: Was that one person, or the whole organization? What did you do the next day?

## Privacy

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): what they will not store, what families ask, and what they already promised in a contract or a handbook.

- What student information are you unwilling to put in a tutoring tool? Why?
- What have parents asked you about where notes, recordings, or names go?
- What have you already promised a school or a family about deletion, access, or retention?
- Tell me about a privacy worry that changed what you used.

Probes: Who handles a deletion request today? How long does it take?

## Contracts and data agreements

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): the paper they already require before a vendor sees student information, and the last time a deal waited on that paper.

- What do you require from a vendor before they can hold student information? Use the last one you signed, or refused to sign.
- What did your last school or district ask you for? What could you not provide?
- Who reviews that paperwork, and how long did it take last time?
- What clause has blocked a tool you otherwise wanted?

Probes: What did you do while you waited? What did you tell the tutors to use instead?

## What would make them switch

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): the job that is painful enough to move, and the cost of moving. We want a switch they made or a switch they refused.

- What are you using now for sessions, notes, and the schedule? What job is each one failing at?
- Tell me about the last time you switched, or seriously tried to. What had to be true first?
- What would you have to move over, and what would you be willing to leave behind?
- What would make you stay with what you have, even if a new tool were better at one job?

Probes: Who would feel the switch? What failed the last time you tried?

## Our open product questions

Ask these last. State each current plan in one plain sentence. Then ask what, if anything, they would change. Do not ask whether they like it.

What we want to learn (do not read this aloud): answers we do not have yet. Write "no change" if that is what they say. Do not push.

- What should happen to the notes when a session is ended early? What, if anything, would you change about ending it with notes, ending it with no notes, or letting it finish first?
- A removed tutor can currently keep read-only access to sessions they taught. Cutting them off completely is a later option. What, if anything, would you change? When would you want the cutoff?
- After the free pilot, ending a session early does not give that time back. It still counts as time used. A session during the pilot is not charged later. What, if anything, would you change? Tell me how you counted the last session that ended early.
- We may later finalize notes automatically at a deadline, as if the tutor had accepted the draft unchanged. What, if anything, would you change about that? Tell me about a draft you would not have wanted saved as-is.
- We are considering daily limits for the whole organization, all staff combined: 20 new learners, 30 claim emails, and 25 staff invites. Mynk could raise a limit or remove it for one organization. What, if anything, would you change?
- When a tutor is removed, or the whole organization is suspended, what should that tutor still be able to finish? For how many days? What should they not be able to finish?

Probes: Use the last time this happened. What did you do? What broke?
